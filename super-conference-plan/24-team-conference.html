24-Team Conference

A conference that consists of 24 teams is where conference scheduling of 10 conference games or less is no longer ideal. For a conference that goes to 24 teams, they would have to strongly consider a conference schedule with at least 11 or 12 games. A 12-game conference schedule plus two non-conference games is the ideal play here, but an 11-game conference schedule with two or three non-conference games could still work, but requires creativity.

Flexible Scheduling

Flexible scheduling can continue with a 24-team conference. However, doing this with only a 9-game or 10-game conference schedule would not be wise. With 24 teams, eitehr an 11-game or 12-game conference schedule with little to no protected annual games would be the play here. Then, the rest of the members would play all the other teams within a 2-year period. For example, if the Big Ten goes to 24 teams, they can only protect the most hated rivalries as annual games, which would be only 12 games. By having the most flexible schedule and not protecting a lot of annual games, you can be creative with how conference schedules can be made. If a team has zero protected opponents, that means that all conference games are rotating opponents. This scheduling flexibility can also apply to any number of conference games.

Pod Scheduling

Pod scheduling can work when managing conference schedules. This time, in a 24-team conference, teams would be split into four pods of six teams. The conference would rotate pods to play all teams in their pod, all teams in one pod, and then one team from another pod based on similar finish of each pod.

Let's do an example of what a hypothetical Big Ten Conference could look like. Let's say the pods are split as the following:

The one opponent from one other pod would be based on similar finishes of the corresponding position of their own pod. For example, a team that finishes first in their pod would play a team that also finish 1st place in their respective pod when not playing other pods in a given year. Let's demonstrate. As ssen here, Ohio State's one other conference opponent is based on their similar finish to their respective pods. For Ohio State, since they would finish 2nd in their pod, North Carolina (who would finish second in the East Pod) would be their other conference opponent. While not a perfect solution, a 24-team conference administering an 12-game conference schedule based on pods would work. However, if not administering 12 conference games, pod scheduling could still work well with an 11-game conference schedule, but there would not be any cross-over pod opponents on years where they don't play two of the three pods. For example, if the West Pod only plays the East Pod, they would not have opponents from the Midwest nor the Great Lakes Pod. Otherwise, you would instead return to the flexibile scheduling scenario.

Even with pod scheduling, you can still have a standing of all the teams based on the conference record. The top team that has the best conference record would be named the conference champion. However, if two or more teams are tied for first, then tie-breakers would be applied.
