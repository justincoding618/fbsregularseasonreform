22-Team Conference

A conference that consists of 22 teams is where conference scheduling of 10 conference games or less is no longer ideal. For a conference that goes to 22 teams, they would have to strongly consider a conference schedule with 12 games. A 12-game conference schedule plus two non-conference games is the ideal play here, but an 11-game conference schedule with two or three non-conference games could still work, but requires creativity.

Possible Conference Scheduling

For an 22-team conference, the possible conference scheduling could look like this.

Flexible Scheduling

A 22-team conference could continue administering a 9-game, 10-game, or 11-game conference schedule. However, once again, they would need to reduce the number of protected annual games to work. For example, in the Big Ten, there are 12 protected annual games. Reducing it down to no more than 9 would be the ideal solution. The only games that would be played annually are their most hated rival. For example, in the Big Ten, the games that should remain protected are The Game (Michigan-Ohio State), Minnesota-Wisconsin, Victory Bell (UCLA-USC), Oregon-Washington, Iowa-Nebraska, Old Oaken Bucket (Indiana-Purdue), Illinois-Northwestern, Maryland-Rutgers, and Michigan State-Penn State. The rest would play all the other teams within a rotational period.

Depending on how many conference games each team would play, here is how the rotational period would go if each member only had one protected opponent:

Number of Conference Games Number of Protected Opponents Rotational Period
9 One for Each Member 5.25-Year Period (Home-and-Home)
10 One for Each Member 4.67-Year Period (Home-and-Home)
11 One for Each Member 4.2-Year Period (Home-and-Home)
12 One for Each Member 2.9-Year Period (Home-and-Home)

This is the most flexible schedule as protecting only the most hated rival of each team means being more creative with how conference schedules. If a team has zero protected opponents, that means that all nine conference games are rotating opponents. This scheduling flexibility can also apply to any number of conference games (including 10 or 11-game conference schedules).

3+9 Schedule

The 3 + 9 schedule would only work with a 12-game conference schedule. Here, each team has three protected opponents while rotating the other opponents. In other words, three out of the 12 conference games were teams to play every season, while the nine remaining games were rotating opponents. Teams would play the other opponents every other year. For a 22-team conference, this is one of the best ways to play all conference opponents within a reasonable time frame. Every team would be able to play all their opponents within a 2-year period. For a home-and-home of every conference opponent, that can be completed within a 4-year period. This scheduling only works for 12 conference games. If going to 11 conference games or less, flexibile scheduling would be strongly recommended.

For a 22-team conference, these are the only possible options that can work. Pod scheduling would not really work here as 22 is not divisible by four. In addition, working with only three pods can become a very tricky scenario. Basically, only two options are available in terms of how a 22-team conference can handle.
