Determining Conference Champions

After the regular season concludes, the conference champion would be determined and no conference championship game would be held. If there is a tie with two or more teams at the top of the conference standings, tie-breakers would go as follows:

  • Head-to-Head
  • Record Against Common Opponents
  • Conference Strength of Schedule
  • Final Regular Season Rankings

Let's break down on how each of them would work.

Head-to-Head

This tie-breaker is the most straight forward. This is where tied teams are analyzed on whether they have beaten each other or not. If there is a tie between two or more teams, the team that has the better record against other tied teams would win the tie-breaker.

2005 Big Ten Season

In 2005, Ohio State and Penn State were named Big Ten co-champions. However, Penn State already defeated Ohio State during the season (but lost to Michigan the next week). Penn State would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over Ohio State and would be named the overall Big Ten champion.

2008 Big Ten Season

In 2008, Ohio State and Penn State once again were named Big Ten co-champions. Like 2005, Penn State already defeated Ohio State during the season (but lost to Iowa the next week). Penn State would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over Ohio State and would be named the overall Big Ten champion.

2006 Pac-10 Season

In 2006, USC and California were named Pac-10 co-champions after finishing with identical 7-2 conference records through a round-robin conference schedule. USC missed out on an outright Pac-10 championship after an upset loss to UCLA. However, USC already defeated California during the season. USC would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over California and would be named the overall Pac-10 champion.

2007 Pac-10 Season

In 2007, USC and Arizona State were named Pac-10 co-champions after finishing with identical 7-2 conference records through a round-robin conference schedule. USC already defeated Arizona State during the season. USC would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over Arizona State and would be named the overall Pac-10 champion.

2012 Big 12 Season

In 2012, Kansas State and Oklahoma both finished with 8-1 conference records after playing a round-robin conference schedule. Both were named Big 12 Co-Champions. Kansas State would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over Oklahoma and would be named the overall Big 12 Champion.

2014 Big 12 Season

In 2014, Baylor and TCU both finished with 8-1 conference records after playing a round-robin conference schedule and were left out of the inaugural 4-team playoff. Both were named Big 12 Co-Champions. Baylor would own the head-to-head tie-breaker over TCU and would be named the overall Big 12 Champion.

2010 Big East Season

Let's look at another example. In 2010, the Big East had a 3-way tie for first place. UConn, West Virginia, and Pittsburgh all finished tied in first place with a 5-2 conference record. The tie-breakers went as follows:

Team Common Opponents
UConn 2-0 vs West Virginia and Pittsburgh
West Virginia 1-1 vs Pittsburgh and UConn
Pittsburgh 0-2 vs UConn and West Virginia

UConn would win the tie-breaker due to the head-to-head sweep of West Virginia and Pittsburgh. West Virginia would be second due to their head-to-head tie-break with Pittsburgh (and despite being ranked in the BCS), while Pittsburgh would finish third. UConn would be named the overall Big East Champion.

2012 Big East Season

Let's look at an example where four teams were tied for first place.In 2012, the Big East had a 4-way tie for first place. Louisville, Cincinnati, Rutgers, and Syracuse tied for first place. The tie-breakers went as follows:

Team Common Opponents
Louisville 2-1 vs Rutgers, Cincinnati, and Syracuse
Rutgers 2-1 vs Louisville, Cincinnati, and Syracuse
Cincinnati 1-2 vs Louisville, Rutgers, and Syracuse
Syracuse 1-2 vs Louisville, Rutgers, and Cincinnati

Louisville and Rutgers went 2-1 in the head-to-head matchups, while Cincinnati and Syracuse went 1-2 in the head-to-head matchups. Cincinnati would finish 3rd overall as it owns the head-to-head tie-breaker over Syracuse. Louisville wins the tie-breaker (as Louisville beat Rutgers head-to-head) and would be named as the overall Big East champion.

1995 WAC Season

Let's look at another example where four teams were tied for first place. In 1995, Colorado State, BYU, Utah, and Air Force all finished with the same conference record at 6-2. Luckily for all four, they all played against each other, so the tie is broken through overall head-to-head record. Here is how all four teams fared:

Team Common Opponents
Colorado State 2-1 vs Air Force, BYU and Utah
Utah 2-1 vs Air Force, BYU and Colorado State
Air Force 1-2 vs BYU, Colorado State, and Utah
BYU 1-2 vs Air Force, Colorado State, and Utah

Air Force and BYU both were on the losing end as they went 1-2 against head-to-head common opponents. However, Air Force would finish 3rd as it owned the head-to-head tiebreaker over BYU. The tie-breaker eliminates Air Force and BYU from being named the overall WAC Champion. Colorado State and Utah went 2-1 against head-to-head common opponents. With Colorado State and Utah still tied, it goes back to head-to-head between two teams. Colorado State would win the overall tie-breaker as it owned the head-to-head tie-breaker over Utah.

1990 Big Ten Season

Let's look at one more example where four teams were tied for first place. In 1990, the Big Ten had a four-way tie for first place. The tie came down to head-to-head. The tie-breaker was the following:

Team Common Opponents
Iowa 3-0 vs Michigan, Michigan State, and Illinois
Michigan State 1-2 vs Iowa, Michigan State, and Illinois
Michigan 1-2 vs Iowa, Michigan State, and Illinois
Illinois 1-2 vs Iowa, Michigan, and Michigan State

Iowa has the head-to-head tie-breaker sweep over Michigan, Michigan State, and Illinois, which means that Iowa would be the overall Big Ten Champion.

Therefore, the tie cannot be broken based on head-to-head.

The next tie-breaker would go to common opponents Wisconsin, Ohio State, and Michigan State played. All three played Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, and Purdue. This is how each of them fared:

With Wisconsin and Ohio State still tied, it goes back to head-to-head between two teams. Wisconsin would be named the overall Big Ten Champion due to their head-to-head tie-breaker over Ohio State.

Conference Strength of Schedule

If the tie cannot be broken through head-to-head nor common opponents, then the next tie-breaker comes down to the conference strength of schedule the tied teams have played. This is especially an important tie-breaker. Let's look at a few examples.

1981 SEC Season

In 1981, the SEC had a weird quirk where all of their teams only played a 6-game conference schedule (which happened until the 1988 season). As a result, the tie could not be broken through head-to-head and common opponents. Alabama and Georgia neither played each other, and both were undefeated against their common opponents. Their common opponents were Kentucky, Vanderbilt, Tennessee, and Auburn. The tie-breaker came down to the conference strength of schedule. Here is how their conference strength of schedule turned out:

Georgia Alabama
W Tennessee (3-3) W LSU (1-4-1)
W Ole Miss (1-4-1) W Kentucky (2-4)
W Vanderbilt (1-5) W Vanderbilt (1-5)
W Kentucky (2-4) W Tennessee (3-3)
W Florida (3-3) W Mississippi State (4-2)
W Auburn (2-4) W Auburn (2-4)
12-23-1 (0.347) 13-22-1 (0.375)

Here, Alabama has the better conference strength than Georgia by one game! Therefore, Alabama would get the SEC's automatic bid.

Alabama, Tennessee, and Auburn played against each other, but were tied head-to-head. In addition, all three were undefeated against their common opponents. Their common opponents were Kentucky and LSU. The tie-breaker came down to the conference strength of schedule. Here is how their conference strength of schedule turned out:

Despite having the same overall regular season record as Tennessee, Alabama has the weakest conference strength of schedule out of the three tied teams. Auburn has the better conference strength than Tennessee by one game! Therefore, Auburn would be named the overall SEC Champion.

If the tie cannot be broken through head-to-head, common opponents, nor conference strength of schedule, then the next tie-breaker comes down to the final regular season rankings of the tied teams. Let's look at a few examples.

In 1996, the Big Ten had a two-way tie between Ohio State and Northwestern. Michigan denied Ohio State the outright Big Ten Championship. However, Northwestern and Ohio State did not play against each other. Let's see how they fared against common opponents:

Surprisingly, Northwestern and Ohio State played the same conference schedule, which means that they are tied in terms of common opponents and conference strength of schedule. 1996 Big East Season

In the Big East, there was a three-way tie between Virginia Tech, Miami (FL), and Syracuse. All three were tied head-to-head, while they were still tied between common opponents and conference strength of schedule. The tie-breaker came down to the final rankings. Since Virginia Tech had the higher final regular season ranking, they would be the overall Big East Champion.

A Doomsday Scenario

The Doomsday Scenario happens if the tie cannot be broken through head-to-head, common opponents, conference strength of schedule, nor final regular season rankings of the tied teams. There is one example where it happened.

1994 SWC Season

In 1994, Texas A&M overwhelmingly finished first place in the Southwest Conference (SWC). However, Texas A&M was ineligible for the conference championship nor postseason due to NCAA sanctions. Therefore, the SWC championship went to the next best team. However, there was a problem. Baylor, Rice, Texas, TCU, and Texas Tech all finished tied with 4-3 conference records. The tie cannot be broken through head-to-head as all five went 2-2 against each other. The tie also cannot be broken through common opponents nor conference strength of schedule as all five played the same round-robin conference schedule. The tie cannot be broken through the final regular season rankings as neither team were ranked in the AP nor Coaches poll.

The SWC had ties to the Cotton Bowl at the time. The tie-breaker came down to the last Cotton Bowl appearance by each team. The last appearances of each tied team were the following:

Team Last Cotton Bowl Appearance
Texas Tech 1938
Rice 1957
TCU 1958
Baylor 1980
Texas 1990

Texas Tech last went to the Cotton Bowl in 1938, which meant that they win the tie-breaker. Texas Tech would go on to lose in blow-out fashion to USC. It would be the final season where the SWC had ties to the Cotton Bowl before the conference itself dissolved after the 1995 season.

Potential Solutions

If we get to a point where neither of the mentioned tie-breakers work on tied teams, there are some potential solutions that can be done. Here are some of my proposed solutions that could resolve the doomsday problems.

Average Computer Rankings

A good way to avoid a doomsday scenario is to break the tie through the average computer rankings between tied teams. This is what the Mountain West Conference did in 2023 and 2025.

In 2023, Boise State, UNLV, and San Jose State all tied with identical 6-2 conference records. While San Jose State played both Boise State and UNLV, Boise State and UNLV neither played against each other. The tie-breaker came down to the average computer rankings from Anderson & Hester, Colley Matrix, Massey, and Wolfe. UNLV had the best average computer rankings, followed by Boise State and San Jose State.

In 2025, Boise State, New Mexico, San Diego State, and UNLV finished in a 4-way tie with identical 6-2 conference records. While Boise State and New Mexico did play all of the other tied teams, San Diego State and UNLV did not play against each other. The tie-breaker once again came down to the average computer rankings, but with different resources this time. The tie-breaker came down to the average ranking from Connelly SP+, ESPN SOR, KPI, and SportSource. Boise State and UNLV had the best average computer rankings out of the tied teams.

Last Playoff Appearance

Another way the tie-breaker can happen is to determine the last playoff appearance by each tied team. This would mean that the team with the longest drought or never appeared in the playoffs would break the tie.

Looking at my 1994-95 playoff field, I gave the playoff bid to Texas Tech. The Southwest Conference gave Texas Tech the tie-breaker due to their last Cotton Bowl appearance. If we went by last playoff appearance, Rice at that point has never appeared in the playoffs. Therefore, Rice would have won the tie-breaker and would have earned their first playoff bid in school history.

However, this tie-breaker is likely not the best way to break the tie. That is because you could have multiple tied teams that never made a playoff appearance in school history. Therefore, you would have to figure out another way to break the tie. Out of the two potential options, the average computer rankings would provide the best way to break the tie. However, if the average computer rankings are not available, then this tie-breaker based on last playoff appearance could work, but can run into trouble if there is two or more tied teams that never made the playoffs in their school history.
