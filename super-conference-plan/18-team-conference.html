18-Team Conference

A conference that consists of 18 teams is where conference scheduling start to become difficult. The Big Ten currently has 18 members. However, playing only half of the members in the conference per year is not very ideal. While a 9-game conference schedule would still work, moving up to a 10-game conference schedule with three non-conference games would be the most ideal. However, an 11-game conference schedule with either two or three non-conference games should not be out of the possibility.

Possible Conference Scheduling

For an 18-team conference, the possible conference scheduling could look like this.

Flexible Scheduling

An 18-team conference could continue administering a 9-game conference schedule. However, they would need to reduce the number of protected annual games to work. For example, in the Big Ten, there are 12 protected annual games. Reducing it down to no more than 9 would be the ideal solution. The only games that would be played annually are their most hated rival. For example, in the Big Ten, the games that should remain protected are The Game (Michigan-Ohio State), Minnesota-Wisconsin, Victory Bell (UCLA-USC), Oregon-Washington, Iowa-Nebraska, Old Oaken Bucket (Indiana-Purdue), Illinois-Northwestern, Maryland-Rutgers, and Michigan State-Penn State. The rest would play all the other teams within a 2-year period. This is the most flexible schedule as protecting only the most hated rival of each team means being more creative with how conference schedules. If a team has zero protected opponents, that means that all nine conference games are rotating opponents. This scheduling flexibility can also apply to any number of conference games (including 10 or 11-game conference schedules).

3+7 Schedule

The 3 + 7 schedule would only work with a 10-game conference schedule. Here, each team has three protected opponents while rotating the other opponents. In other words, three out of the ten conference games were teams to play every season, while the seven remaining games were rotating opponents. Teams would play the other opponents every other year. For an 18-team conference, this is one of the best ways to play all conference opponents within a reasonable time frame. Every team would be able to play all their opponents within a 2-year period. For a home-and-home of every conference opponent, that can be completed within a 4-year period. This scheduling only works for 10 conference games. If staying at 9 conference games or increasing the conference schedule to more than 10 conference games, flexibile scheduling would be strongly recommended.

For an 18-team conference, these are the only possible options that can work. Pod scheduling would not really work here as 18 is not divisible by four. In addition, working with only three pods can become a very tricky scenario. Basically, only two options are available in terms of how an 18-team conference can handle.
