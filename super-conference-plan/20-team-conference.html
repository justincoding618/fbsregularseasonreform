20-Team Conference

A conference that consists of 20 teams is where conference scheduling of 9 conference games or less is no longer ideal. For a conference that goes to 20 teams, a 9-game conference schedule would not be wise. They would have to strongly consider a conference schedule with at least 10 games. An 11-game conference schedule with two or three non-conference games should not be out of the possibility as well.

Possible Conference Scheduling

For a 20-team conference, the possible conference scheduling could look like this.

Flexible Scheduling

Flexible scheduling can continue with a 20-team conference. However, doing this with only a 9-game conference schedule would not be wise. With 20 teams, a 10-game conference schedule with little to no protected annual games would be the play here. Then, the rest of the members would play all the other teams within a 2-year period. For example, if the Big Ten goes to 20 teams, they can only protect the most hated rivalries as annual games, which would be only ten games.

Depending on how many conference games each team would play, here is how the rotational period would go if each member only had one protected opponent:

Number of Conference Games Number of Protected Opponents Rotational Period
9 One for Each Member 4.75-Year Period (Home-and-Home)
10 One for Each Member 4.44-Year Period (Home-and-Home)
11 One for Each Member 3.8-Year Period (Home-and-Home)

By having the most flexible schedule and not protecting a lot of annual games, you can be creative with how conference schedules can be made. If a team has zero protected opponents, that means that all conference games are rotating opponents. This scheduling flexibility can also apply to any number of conference games (including an 11-game conference schedule).

3+8 Schedule

If a 20-team conference wants each team to have three annual opponents, then going to an 11-game conference schedule is the ideal solution. Here, three out of the 11 conference games are teams to play every season, while the eight remaining games are rotating opponents. Teams would play the other opponents every other year. For a 20-team conference, this is one of the best ways to play all conference opponents within a reasonable time frame. Every team would be able to play all their opponents within a 2-year period. For a home-and-home of every conference opponent, that can be completed within a 4-year period. This scheduling only works for 11 conference games. If moving to any conference schedule that is not 11 conference games, flexibile scheduling would be strongly recommended.

Pod Scheduling

Pod scheduling can work when managing conference schedules. This time, in a 20-team conference, teams would be split into four pods of five teams. The conference would rotate pods to play all teams in their pod, all teams in one pod, and then one team from all the other pods based on similar finish of each pod.

The one opponent from the two other pods would be based on similar finishes of the corresponding position of their own pod. For example, a team that finishes first in their pod would play teams that also finish 1st place in their respective pods when not playing other pods in a given year. Let's demonstrate. While not a perfect solution, a 20-team conference administering an 11-game conference schedule based on pods would work.

While not a perfect solution, a 20-team conference administering an 10-game conference schedule based on pods would work. However, if not administering 10 or 11 conference games, pod scheduling could still work well with a 9-game conference schedule, but there would not be any cross-over pod opponents on years where they don't play two of the three pods. For example, if the West Pod only plays the East Pod, they would not have opponents from the Midwest nor the Great Lakes Pod. Otherwise, you would instead return to the flexibile scheduling scenario.

Even with pod scheduling, you can still have a standing of all the teams based on the conference record. The top team that has the best conference record would be named the conference champion. However, if two or more teams are tied for first, then tie-breakers would be applied.
