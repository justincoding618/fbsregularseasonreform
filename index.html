FBS Regular Season Reform Overview

When Division I split football programs as I-A (later FBS) and I-AA (later FCS), all conferences were small conferences that geographically made sense. Each conference supported no more than ten teams, and at least some of the conferences play a round-robin schedule. As conferences realign, some conferences survive, and others dissolve or barely survive by backfilling. Since 2024, Division I FBS has entered the super power conference era. Three out of the four remaining power conferences go from coast-to-coast (spanning in nearly all of the different time zones), with the SEC being the only conference that geographically makes sense. Teams leave their old conference homes to join their new conference homes for certain reasons. Conferences have done their best to make sure all teams play all conference opponents within a given period. However, as the size of a given conference grows, conference schedules become unbalanced, where not all teams would play the top of the competition within their respective conferences.

The Problem

No matter the conference size, there are some years where a team's conference schedule is easier, and other years where a team's conference schedule is tougher. Super conferences provide incentive for teams to win their conferences despite easier conference schedules, while a conference that becomes too small could result in regular season scheduling problems. Conferences need to meet the mandatory minimum of eight teams to operate in Division I FBS conference. Although the mandatory minimum does provide some structure, there is currently no maximum limit to how many members a conference can have. The Protect College Sports Act does provide a maximum 20-team limit for all conferences, but it's not guaranteed to pass. No matter the solution, an answer to resolve these problems is a way to make sure conference schedules can become balanced for every season. If necessary, conferences may need to be downsized in order for schedules to be reasonable.

The Main Solution

The main solution would do the following:

  • Incentivize Round-Robin Conference Schedules: All conferences would meet the max universal size and would play a round-robin conference schedule
  • Regular Season: Depending on the universal conference sizes, regular season could be a 12-game, 13-game, or 14-game regular season schedule
  • Max Regular Season Limit: If Super Conferences remain, there would be a max 12-game conference schedule with two non-conference games for teams in each super conference
  • Preserves Tradition: This solution does its best to try to maintain each team's most hated rival to end the regular season
  • Regular Season Matters: Seeding determines home games, bowl selection, and matchup paths
